# Plan — *Cymodocea rotundata* only

An agent that builds this species reads this file and no other species plan. Do not edit other species folders, their `.glb` files, or their lesson videos.

Slug: `cymodocea_rotundata`. Prefix: `CR`. Common name: smooth ribbon seagrass.

This is the reference meadow. It stays a smooth ribbon. It must not pick up a serrated tip, a trident, oval leaves, or a round leaf.

## Not the same plant as the others

This is a medium strap meadow. Do not reuse it as the body for serrulata, Halodule, Thalassia, Syringodium, Halophila, or Enhalus.

Presentation: the lesson camera sits close on a leaf tip and then on the closed scar ring. The patch is several shoots along one smooth rhizome, straps of mixed length, not a single straight row.

Roots: fine roots leaving the nodes and spreading in the sand. The rhizome stays smooth. Do not add bristles or cord-like roots.

Seabed: use the shared uneven bed (mounds, dips, small ridges). Do not build a flat sand sheet.

## What a person must see

- Flat strap, rounded smooth tip, no teeth.
- Sheath scars form a closed ring around the short stem.
- Smooth rhizome. Roots down at the nodes.
- Strap width 4 mm, the top of the Seagrass-Watch 2–4 mm range. Do not speak a vein count.

The look-alike is *C. serrulata* (wider strap, serrated tip, scars that do not close). If this model could be mistaken for that plant, it is not done.

## Lesson

30 seconds, 1920×1080. Remove the floating 3D text plates. Subtitles sit along the bottom and match the speech. No music.

Voice: a teacher, not one flat rate. Pause between ideas. Slow down on “rounded” and “closed ring”.

Spoken script, also the `transcript` for this slug in `app/assets/data/species.json`:

This is smooth ribbon seagrass, Cymodocea rotundata. What you are seeing is a display, not a scan of a meadow in Mati.

Look at the tip. It is rounded and smooth. There are no teeth along the edge.

On the short stem, the sheath scars join into a closed ring. That ring is how you tell this plant from serrated ribbon seagrass, which does not close its scars.

The patch spreads when the buried stem grows through the sand and sends up new shoots. Roots grow down at each node. The plant can also flower and set seed.
