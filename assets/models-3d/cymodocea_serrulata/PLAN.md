# Plan — *Cymodocea serrulata* only

An agent that builds this species reads this file and no other species plan. Do not open or overwrite `assets/models-3d/cymodocea_rotundata/`.

Slug: `cymodocea_serrulata`. Prefix: `CS`. Common name: serrated ribbon seagrass.

Private builders: `assets/models-3d/cymodocea_serrulata_build/`. Blender: `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe` in the background. Not the live Blender window.

## Not the same plant as smooth ribbon

Wider straps than *C. rotundata*, a different tip, a different sheath, and scars that do not close. If the two meadows can be swapped without anyone noticing, this one is not done.

Presentation: open on the serrated tip, then the triangular sheath, then a stem whose scars do not meet. Leaves are broader and the patch reads heavier than the smooth ribbon meadow.

Roots: fine roots at the nodes. Do not copy Enhalus cords or Thalassia’s thick scarred rhizome.

Seabed: the shared uneven bed only. Do not make a flat plate, and do not design a private sand style.

## What a person must see

This must not look like smooth ribbon seagrass.

- Flat strap, 9 mm wide, the top of the Seagrass-Watch 5–9 mm range. Do not copy the 4 mm strap.
- Serrated tip. Tooth count and tooth size are not spoken and are not written as facts.
- Sheath broadly triangular.
- Leaf scars do not meet around the stem. Not a closed ring.
- No counted vein grooves. Do not copy 11 veins.

## Lesson

30 seconds, 1920×1080. Remove the floating 3D text plates. Subtitles along the bottom, the same words as the speech. No music.

Voice: pause between ideas. Slow down on “serrated” and “do not form a closed ring”. Do not read in one flat rate.

Spoken script, also the `transcript` for this slug only:

This is serrated ribbon seagrass, Cymodocea serrulata. It is a display, not a scan of a meadow in Mati.

The leaf is a flat strap, wider than smooth ribbon seagrass. Run your eye to the tip. The edge is serrated.

The sheath is broadly triangular. Then look at the stem. The leaf scars do not form a closed ring. That open scar line is how you separate this plant from smooth ribbon seagrass.

The patch spreads as the buried stem grows through the sand. Roots grow down at each node. It can also flower and set seed.

Do not speak a tooth count, a vein count, a stem height, or a leaf length.
