## Not a ribbon seagrass

Fine leaves, one dark vein, black scars on a pale rhizome. It must look thinner and paler than either *Cymodocea*, and the tip must not match *H. uninervis*.

Presentation: the lesson moves in on the rounded tip until the split vein is obvious, then down to the black scars. Do not use the serrulata camera or the strap width.

Roots: fine roots at the nodes on a pale rhizome. The black scars are on that rhizome. No bristles.

Seabed: the shared uneven bed only.

## What a person must see

This is the rounded needle, not the trident.

- Fine strap. Leaf width is not in the record. A display width may live in CONFIG. Do not speak it.
- One dark central vein that splits in two at a rounded tip.
- Pale rhizome with clean black leaf scars.
- The tip must not be a trident. If it can be confused with *H. uninervis*, it is not done.

## Lesson

30 seconds, 1920×1080. No floating 3D text plates. Subtitles along the bottom. No music.

Voice: pause before the tip sentence. Slow down on “splits” and “rounded”. Not one flat rate.

Spoken script, also this slug’s `transcript` only:

This is needle seagrass, Halodule pinifolia. A display, not a scan of a meadow in Mati.

Each leaf is fine, with one dark vein down the centre. Watch the tip. That vein splits in two, and the tip stays rounded.

That rounded tip is how you tell it from the other needle seagrass, Halodule uninervis, whose tip is a trident.

The rhizome is usually pale, with clean black leaf scars. The patch spreads when the buried stem grows through the sand. Roots grow down at each node. It can also flower and set seed.

Do not speak a leaf width. The record’s “up to about 20 cm” may be mentioned as the published length. Do not present a model length as a Mati measurement.
