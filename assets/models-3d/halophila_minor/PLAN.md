# Plan — *Halophila minor* only

An agent that builds this species reads this file and no other species plan. Do not edit `halophila_ovalis`. Do not give this plant eight or more cross veins.

Slug: `halophila_minor`. Prefix: `HM`. Common name: small spoon seagrass.

Private builders: `assets/models-3d/halophila_minor_build/`. Blender: `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe` in the background.

## Not a strap and not ovalis

Paired small ovals lying off the rhizome. No upright fan. Fewer cross veins and a smaller leaf than *H. ovalis*. A line of straps is the wrong plant.

Presentation: look down onto the pairs so the ovals and the wedge sheath read. Keep the camera close. A far shot turns this patch into one line.

Roots: fine roots at the nodes. Leaves come from the rhizome, not from a tall stem. No bristles.

Seabed: the shared uneven bed only. The little leaves should sit in the dips and rises, not on a flat sheet.

## What a person must see

This is not a strap fan and not *H. ovalis*.

- Small oval leaves in pairs, arising from the rhizome. No upright fan of straps.
- Smooth margin. Wedge-shaped sheath.
- Fewer than eight pairs of cross veins. A display count of 5, 6, or 7 may sit in CONFIG. Do not speak that count. Say “fewer than eight”.
- Leaf length, width, and petiole length are OPEN and are not spoken.

The current phone model reads as one line because the leaves are tiny and the camera sits too far back. The lesson meadow is the one to export, framed close enough that the paired ovals read as a patch.

## Lesson

30 seconds, 1920×1080. No floating 3D text plates. Subtitles along the bottom. No music.

Voice: pause before the vein sentence. Slow down on “pairs” and “fewer than eight”.

Spoken script, also this slug’s `transcript` only:

This is small spoon seagrass, Halophila minor. A display, not a scan of a meadow in Mati.

The leaves come up in pairs. Each one is a small oval, straight from the buried stem. There is no upright fan. The margin is smooth, and the sheath is wedge-shaped.

The cross veins are fewer than eight pairs. That smaller leaf, and those fewer veins, are how you separate it from spoon seagrass, Halophila ovalis.

The patch spreads as the buried stem grows. Roots grow down at the nodes. It can also flower and set seed.
